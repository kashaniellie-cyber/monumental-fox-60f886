Founder

GeoMera was born from my deep appreciation for the timeless wisdom embodied in the world's great historical architecture.

Standing before places such as Machu Picchu, the Pyramids, and other extraordinary works of human achievement, I found myself drawn beyond their beauty to the intelligence that made them possible. They revealed a profound understanding of land, geometry, proportion, climate, nature, and the people they were created to serve. Their significance was never the result of a single idea, but of many disciplines brought together with remarkable coherence.

Born in Persia, I was fortunate to grow up within one of the world's most distinguished architectural traditions. Persian gardens, the urban fabric of Isfahan, traditional courtyards, and ingenious windcatchers reflected a culture in which geometry, water, light, climate, and nature were composed as one. That heritage shaped the way I came to understand the relationship between people and place.

My work in real estate across three continents offered another perspective. I witnessed land transformed into homes, communities, and destinations, each carrying the potential to influence the lives of generations. It became increasingly clear that while architecture continues to evolve, the principles behind the world's most enduring places remain as relevant today as they have always been.

This is not knowledge preserved only in history. It continues to inform the work of architects, landscape architects, geomancers, environmental specialists, and other practitioners who understand that exceptional places emerge from the thoughtful integration of many disciplines, guided by a profound understanding of the land itself.

That understanding became the foundation of GeoMera.

The ambition has never been to recreate the past or define a particular architectural style. It is to ensure that the knowledge which shaped many of history's greatest places continues to inform the creation of extraordinary places for generations to come.

— Founder, Ellie Kashani

What I Believe

Land speaks first. Architecture listens.

Every proportion should feel inevitable, never arbitrary.

Craft is not a finishing touch. It is the foundation.

Working with fewer clients, more closely, is not a limitation. It is the point.

Orientation is never incidental. The greatest civilizations positioned entire cities by the sun, the wind, and the stars.

The Golden Ratio is not ornament. It is a proportion the eye recognizes as true before the mind can explain why.

Water, light, and shade are structural elements. The windcatcher understood this centuries before mechanical cooling did.

No place endures on architecture alone. It takes geomancy, landscape, engineering, and craft moving as one discipline.
